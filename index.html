A Light on Shakespeare Road

For Pat Fish

I first met Pat at a photo shoot organised by Northampton's short-lived but much-loved Music For Squares magazine. The idea was that local musicians would dress up as their favourite rock/pop star for the day: Freddie Mercury, Bruce Springsteen, Jimi Hendrix, etc. I went as Townes Van Zandt, can of Coke (full-fat) in one hand and bottle of Jack Daniel's in the other. Go and sit next to Kevin Ayers, I'm told.

And so began my friendship with Pat Fish, The Jazz Butcher, last of the gentleman adventurers, hard-water kid of the asbestos age, and the only person in the room who didn't need to pretend to be a rock and roll legend.

I don't have a hoard of songs from the last 15 years. I often wonder why. I think I probably just stopped looking for them. But of course, some songs find you, and they are usually the ones that fuck you up.

More people heard Making History for the first time in the warmth of Pat's living room than probably anywhere else. So it seems fitting to dedicate its re-release to him.

I guess, like a lot of people, the list of things I wish I'd asked Pat while he was still on this planet gets longer every day, but the one thing I want to say is: thank you, Pat xx.
